Privacy

Tilde · LaTeX editor · last changed 16 November 2026

In short: Tilde has no server and no database. Your documents go from your browser to your own Google Drive and nowhere else. Tilde never receives your files, your Google account details or anything you type, and it uses no analytics, no advertising and no cookies of its own.

What Tilde is

Tilde is a free, open-source LaTeX editor. It is a set of static files; everything, including compiling, runs in your own browser. Its source code is public on GitHub, so anyone can check what it does.

Google sign-in and Google Drive

  • Signing in is only needed to keep projects in Google Drive. The examples work without it.
  • Tilde asks Google for one permission, drive.file: to see, create, change and delete only the files that Tilde creates or that you choose in Google's file picker. It cannot see anything else in your Drive.
  • Tilde uses this only to save and open your projects in your own Drive, and to note small settings on its own folders and files (such as a project's main file).
  • Google gives Tilde an access token that lasts about an hour. It stays in your browser tab and is gone when you close the tab or sign out.
  • Tilde reads three values about your account from Google: an anonymous account number, used in your browser to keep unsaved edits apart if several people use the same computer, and your name and the address of your profile photo as Google shows them, which co-authors of a shared project see (below). It does not read your email address or contacts.
  • In a shared project, the People panel shows who the project is shared with: their names, email addresses, profile photos and whether they may edit or only view, as Google Drive lists them. Tilde reads this list only to show it in your browser.

Shared projects

When you share a project, or join one someone shared with you:

  • The project's folder is shared through Google Drive with the people you name; Google emails them a link. People invited to edit can change its files; people invited to view can read and compile them.
  • Tilde keeps three of its own items in the project's folder: the project's text, which merges everyone's edits and holds the comments written in it, each with the name of who wrote it (resolving a comment deletes it), and files deleted in the last 30 days, with the name of who deleted them; a mailbox file with the names and profile photo addresses of the people who have the project open right now; and a folder for images that co-authors add.
  • While two people have the project open, their browsers may connect directly to each other, to send typing and cursor positions at once. Each then sees the other's IP address, as with any direct connection, and Google's free STUN server (stun.l.google.com) learns your IP address to help make the connection. The connection is encrypted, and nothing passes through a server of Tilde's: Tilde has none.
  • Leaving a project removes it from your list. To stop sharing a project with someone, its owner removes them in the People panel or in Google Drive.

Sharing a PDF by link

Only when you click Share PDF above the PDF and confirm, Tilde saves that PDF in the project's folder in your Drive and lets anyone with its link view it, without signing in. Updating it replaces the PDF behind the same link. Stop sharing it ends the link and moves the PDF to your Drive's trash.

What Tilde keeps in your browser

  • Unsaved edits, until they reach your Drive, so a closed tab or a lost network loses nothing (for a shared project, its whole text). Signing out deletes them, after offering to download anything not yet saved.
  • The TeX engine and TeX package files, so later compiles are fast and work offline. These contain nothing of yours.
  • Small settings: light or dark, pane sizes, PDF zoom, auto-compile, how the project list is sorted, and which project was open last.

All of this stays on your computer. Your browser's "clear site data" removes it.

What goes to other services, and when

  • Google, for sign-in, Google Drive and the file picker, and in shared projects for finding a direct connection (STUN), under Google's privacy policy.
  • Your co-authors' browsers, in a shared project you have open at the same time: your edits, cursor position and name, directly.
  • TeXlyre's TeX package server (texlive2026.texlyre.org): the names of TeX package files that a document needs and that are not built in, such as TikZ. Never the document itself.
  • doi.org, only when you add a paper by its DOI: that DOI. doi.org may pass the request on to the service that holds the citation, such as Crossref or DataCite.
  • The website host: like any website, the server that delivers Tilde's files sees your IP address. Tilde is hosted on GitHub Pages, under GitHub's privacy statement.

Tilde does not sell, share or use your information for anything else.

Google API Services User Data Policy

Tilde's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

Removing your data

  • Your projects are ordinary folders in your Google Drive. Delete them there whenever you like.
  • To take away Tilde's access, open your Google account's third-party connections and remove Tilde.
  • Signing out of Tilde deletes what it keeps for you in the browser.
